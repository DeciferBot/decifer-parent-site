# This repository is the marketing site. Read this before writing copy.

decifer.io is a lead generation asset. It has exactly two jobs:

1. **Pull traffic in.** Search, shares, forwarded links.
2. **Convert that traffic into a qualified enquiry.** A named person with a
   real problem, in the inbox.

**A page that does not do one of those two things is failing at its job.**
Every page, every article, every tool, every section within them. There is no
"just informational" surface here.

## Keep the delivery rules out of the marketing

This is the mistake to avoid, and it has already been made once in this repo.

- `PRODUCT.md`, `rules.md` and the service pages describe **how Decifer builds
  systems for clients**: scope written in advance, do-not-automate lists,
  removing a model when plain code is better, telling a client the truth
  inside a paid engagement. That discipline is the **product**. It is what the
  site sells and describes.
- It is **not** a rule set for how the site sells. Delivery honesty is owed to
  a client who has paid. A visitor who has paid nothing is owed a useful,
  accurate page, not a recommendation to keep their money.

Do not import build-time caution into marketing copy. Specifically: never
write site copy that tells a visitor not to buy, not to build, not to launch,
or to leave it for now. That is a delivery conversation, and it happens after
the enquiry, on a call, where it is worth something.

## Rules for anything on this site

- **State facts, recommend action.** Numbers, scores and arithmetic are
  reported exactly as they come out, including when they are unflattering.
  What follows them is always what it would take to make the thing work, never
  a verdict to walk away. Accurate is required; discouraging is not.
- **Every interaction ends somewhere.** A verdict, a score, a calculation, an
  article, a case: hand the reader a next step that fits what they just told
  you, not a generic footer. A dead end is a lost lead however good the
  content is.
- **Carry the intent forward.** If a reader has already told the site
  something (numbers in a tool, the process they care about, the article they
  read), carry it into the next screen instead of asking again. Every field
  they retype costs conversions.
- **Route by outcome.** A weak result and a strong one convert differently.
  Send each somewhere that fits: a call, the fixed-fee assessment, a smaller
  first build, a service page.
- **Traffic side counts too.** Titles, descriptions, headings, structured data
  and internal links are part of the job. A page nobody can find fails the
  first test whatever it says.

## What stays

Credibility is what makes these enquiries qualified: real numbers with a
source, real proof, plain English, no hype. Keep all of it. The line is
between being **truthful**, which is required, and being **discouraging**,
which is not our job on this side of the sale.
